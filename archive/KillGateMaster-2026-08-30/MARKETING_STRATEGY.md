# Killgate Marketing Strategy

**Purpose:** Create demand for Killgate without pretending that it can predict a startup's future. This plan positions it as the decision-quality alternative to generic AI idea graders, then turns that positioning into a compliant Google Play listing, a landing-page funnel, launch campaigns, and a measurable growth loop.

**Working product facts used here:** Killgate creates a locked Validation Contract before live research; produces a free Brief that shows the exact work order but not a disguised free report; runs source-led, claim-to-source research with disconfirming-evidence checks; maintains an audit trail; and applies a deterministic gate in which AI cannot override the outcome. Current offers are a $19.99 Venture Pass for three research passes on one idea family, a $7.99 same-family extra run, and a $59.99/month Founder Pro plan for nine monthly passes, plus a Pro-only top-up. Confirm these facts in the released build and Play catalog before publishing any copy.

## 1. The commercial thesis

### The job to be done

A serious early-stage founder is not primarily buying a business plan, a prettier pitch deck, or permission to feel optimistic. They are trying to answer:

> "What evidence would make this idea worth my next 100 hours or $10,000 — and what evidence should make me stop?"

Killgate should own that question. It is a **venture decision system**, not an "AI idea generator," a generic chatbot, or an instant scorecard.

### The concise positioning

**Killgate helps founders decide what to build, pivot, or kill before the costly build begins — with a locked test, source-led research, and an auditable decision trail.**

### Brand promise

**Kill weak ideas early. Build the survivors.**

This is strong but defensible because Killgate can return a failing or incomplete outcome instead of manufacturing a favorable score. Never turn it into a guarantee of success, a claim that Killgate "knows" an idea will win, or a claim that it replaces customer interviews.

### Who it is for first

| Priority | Segment | Moment of urgency | Message that should land |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solo SaaS/mobile-app founders with a specific idea | They are about to spend nights, savings, or contractor budget on an MVP. | "Before you build the first feature, set the evidence bar." |
| 2 | Service-business operators productizing a repeated problem | They see a pain point at work but are unsure it is a scalable business. | "Separate a real operational pain from a product people will pay for." |
| 3 | Serial founders and independent consultants | They screen several opportunities and need a repeatable method. | "Use the same decision discipline across every opportunity." |
| 4 | Accelerators, founder communities, and entrepreneurship programs | They need a structured pre-work tool, not a false substitute for mentoring. | "Give every founder a clear validation work order before the next office hour." |

Do not lead with students, casual "idea generators," or people seeking an investor deck. They may use the product, but they dilute the decisive, evidence-first positioning and tend to expect unlimited free output.

## 2. What the category says — and how Killgate wins without naming it

The current founder-validation category commonly promotes instant viability scores, broad market/competitor summaries, many AI-generated report sections, free first scans, and bundles that continue into branding, plans, decks, or marketing assets. Those are convenient promises, but they create a trust gap: a founder can receive a polished answer without knowing what would disprove it.

Killgate is superior **for a founder who values a defensible decision over instant reassurance**. The distinction should be explained, not asserted as an unqualified ranking.

| Common category promise | The buyer's quiet doubt | Killgate's better experience | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Get a score in seconds" | What actually moved the score, and can I act on it? | Start with a locked Validation Contract and show the gates, assumptions, and work order. | The founder sees the test before seeing the result. That makes the next action inspectable rather than mysterious. |
| "AI-generated report" | Is this research or well-formatted opinion? | Research is source-led: discovery is classified, evidence must meet claim-to-source rules, and the result has an audit trail. | A founder can trace the decision and discuss the evidence with a cofounder, mentor, or buyer. |
| "Confidence to build" | Is the tool trying to encourage me because that sells? | Killgate can direct a founder to continue validating, pivot, or kill; AI cannot override the deterministic decision gate. | A negative outcome can save months of build time. Honest friction is more valuable than false momentum. |
| "One massive report" | What should I do first, and what does a rerun mean? | The free Brief identifies the exact question; paid passes are tied to the same idea family and preserve a decision history. | The product supports an iterative investigation, not repeated attempts to obtain a friendlier answer. |
| "Free scan" | Is the free result useful, or just a teaser? | The no-cost Brief is explicit about what it is: the instrument and work order, not hidden live evidence or a fake verdict. | Clear scope builds trust before purchase and protects research economics. |
| "More AI agents / more pages" | Does more output mean more proof? | Killgate sells a clearer next decision, not raw report volume. | It reduces the common failure mode of reading a long document and still not knowing whether to proceed. |

### The proof hierarchy for all copy

Use claims in this order. The closer a message stays to the top, the stronger and safer it is.

1. **Demonstrable workflow:** locked contract, explicit gates, source-led research, audit trail, decision states, data export.
2. **User benefit:** know what must be true before investing more; avoid unexamined assumptions; leave with a next action.
3. **Careful outcome language:** helps founders make a more disciplined decision; can surface reasons to continue, pivot, or stop.
4. **Never claim:** guaranteed startup success, exhaustive market coverage, objectively correct prediction, investor approval, "the best," or unnamed aggregate performance statistics that have not been measured.

## 4. Google Play launch package

### Store category and tags

- **Primary category:** Business.
- **Support tags to evaluate in Play Console:** entrepreneurship, small business, productivity, business planning, market research. Select only tags the live app and its screenshots genuinely support.
- **Do not position it as finance, investment advice, or an investor-guarantee product.** It helps with venture research and decisions; it does not recommend securities or promise funding.

### Recommended title

**Killgate: Venture Decisions**

This stays within Play's 30-character title limit, includes the brand, and makes the category legible. Retain the in-app short name **Killgate**.

### Play short description (69 characters)

**Test business ideas with evidence, clear gates, and a decision trail.**

This is under the 80-character Play limit. Do not add price, "free," ranking language, guarantees, or competitor references.

### Play full description

Killgate helps founders decide whether an idea deserves more time, money, and building.

Start with a free Killgate Brief. It turns your idea into a locked Validation Contract: the hypothesis, critical assumptions, evidence that counts, and the gates that can still fail. You see the test before you pay for live research.

When you run research, Killgate looks for relevant public evidence, records the sources behind it, and keeps an audit trail of the decision. It is designed to surface support, risks, contradictions, and the questions that still need real customer conversations.

Killgate does not treat an AI score as permission to build. Its decision process can point to continued validation, a pivot, or stopping the idea. AI-assisted research cannot override the decision gate.

What you can do:

- Turn a plain-English business idea into a structured validation brief.
- Lock the hypothesis so the test does not move with the answer.
- Review critical assumptions, evidence standards, and research questions.
- Run source-led research with claim-to-source checks and disconfirming-evidence search.
- Keep a private audit trail of evidence and decisions.
- Export your validation data and manage or delete your account in the app.

Killgate is built for solo founders, operators, and consultants who want a clearer next decision before they invest in an MVP, agency work, or a launch.

What Killgate does not do:

- It does not guarantee that a business will succeed.
- It does not replace conversations with real customers.
- It does not offer unlimited research.

The free Brief shows the validation method and work order. Live research is available through a Venture Pass for one idea family, with additional same-project runs and a Founder Pro option for repeat users. Prices shown in Google Play are localized and may vary by region.

Build less on hope. Decide with evidence.

Capture an actual Android/TWA experience before upload, then produce localized variants for every language supported.

### Feature graphic concept

Use the existing Killgate mark and light background. Center a large, simple gate motif with three small, non-score labels arranged beneath it: **Evidence · Decision · Next move**. Do not use a phone screenshot, a numerical score dial, price, a Play badge, or tiny explanatory copy. The graphic should feel like product identity, not a display ad.

### Store-listing compliance checklist

- Every feature mentioned is visible in the public build or clearly reachable after sign-in/purchase.
- The listing never promises successful funding, revenue, a winning startup, guaranteed market demand, or an infallible score.
- Any future quote includes the real speaker's name/role, permission, and a direct attribution; do not use anonymous, composite, or invented testimonials.
- Screenshots show Android-compatible, current UI and match the title/description.
- Text avoids prices/promotions in the title, short description, feature graphic, and screenshots. Put only truthful, localized price context in the long description and Play billing screens.
- Re-check policy and field limits in Play Console immediately before submission because platform rules can change.

Google's current guidance requires an accurate description, an 80-character short description, a 4,000-character full description, and no unsubstantiated or anonymous testimonial claims. It also advises that the top portion of the description carry the important value message. See [Google Play store-listing guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en) and [Google Play discovery guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/4448378?hl=en).

## 5. Landing page and conversion path

### Above the fold

**Headline:** Before you build, set the evidence bar.

**Subhead:** Killgate turns your business idea into a locked validation test, then gives you source-led research and a clear next decision — continue, pivot, or stop.

**Primary CTA:** Create your free Brief

**Secondary CTA:** View a sample validation

**Proof strip:** Locked contract · Source-led research · Decision trail · No unlimited AI fluff

The hero should show a real Validation Contract or decision view. A sophisticated glowing-AI visual is weaker than a recognizable product moment.

### Page order

1. **The cost of building before testing:** Frame lost build time and outside spend without fabricated statistics.
2. **How Killgate works:** Idea → locked Brief → live research → evidence gate → next action.
3. **Why a score is not a decision:** Explain that a score without an evidence standard is difficult to challenge; contrast with the contract/audit trail.
4. **See the deliverable:** Embed one approved sample report and label it as a sample, not a customer outcome.
5. **Pricing with scope clarity:** Venture Pass as the default; Founder Pro visibly for serial founders/consultants; no confusing comparison table built around raw output volume.
6. **What it cannot establish:** Public research is not customer proof. This surprisingly increases trust and primes the customer-interview step.
7. **FAQ and privacy:** Explain data ownership/export/deletion, the meaning of a pass, how pivots stay tied to an idea family, and that results are decision support rather than a guarantee.

### Activation sequence

| Moment | Trigger | Message | Success event |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 minutes | Account created | "Describe the customer, painful problem, change, and price assumption." | Specific idea saved |
| Immediately after Brief | Contract generated | "Before research: does this say what you actually mean? Lock it when it does." | Contract reviewed/locked |
| First research paywall | Gated live evidence | "You have the work order. A Venture Pass runs up to three research passes for this idea family." | Purchase view opened |
| Result delivered | A gate is returned | "Do not just read this. Choose the next evidence action: interview, feasibility check, pivot, or stop." | Next action recorded |
| 3–7 days later | No next action after result | "Your result is waiting for a decision. What evidence would change your mind?" | Return session / action completed |

Messages must be based on actual user behavior, honor consent/preferences, and never imply that a result guarantees success.

### Organic content pillars

1. **Evidence anatomy:** Explain what counts as a meaningful signal versus a clever anecdote or unverified search result.
2. **Kill criteria:** "What would make you stop?" posts. This is the most distinctive, contrarian content pillar.

5. **Method transparency:** Explain why the hypothesis locks, why an AI cannot overrule the gate, and why public research does not replace buyer conversations.

## 7. Pricing narrative and purchase design

### Sell the unit of value, not raw AI output

Lead with **one idea family, three deliberate research passes**. The customer is not buying tokens, agents, or pages. They are buying an investigation that can advance through evidence without changing the question after the answer arrives.

| Offer | Role in the story | Recommended framing |
|---|---|---|
| Free Killgate Brief | Trust and activation | "See the exact test before you buy the evidence." |
| Venture Pass | Default decision purchase | "Three research passes for the business idea you are actually testing." |
| Extra Evidence Run | Continuation, not an upsell trick | "One more check on this same project when a new question emerges." |
| Founder Pro | Repeat-user/consultant plan | "For people screening opportunities regularly — not a default plan for every founder." |
| Pro top-up | Explicit overage control | "Choose more capacity only when you need it; no surprise overages." |

Avoid anchoring Venture Pass against a made-up consultant cost or publishing a dollar-savings claim until it can be substantiated. The conversion argument is the cost of unexamined build time, not "cheap AI."

## 8. Launch sequence

### Phase 0 — credibility setup (2 weeks)

- Run 20–30 observed beta validations with founders who consent to feedback research. Measure comprehension, not just satisfaction.
- Identify the three screens that best explain the contract, evidence, and decision gate; refine those before paid acquisition.
- Publish privacy, support, account deletion, and pricing pages; test the signup and purchase path end-to-end on the internal Play track.
- Collect only verified, attributed quotes after users have completed a real workflow. If there are no quotes, launch without social proof; the product method is the proof.
- Prepare 2–3 **clearly labeled illustrative** sample validations from non-confidential, invented scenarios, plus the existing approved samples.

### Phase 1 — controlled public launch (weeks 1–4)

- Launch Play listing and web landing page with the same promise and product screenshots.
- Start organic method-led content and 3–5 partner/community pilots.
- Run small paid-search tests only after analytics can attribute install → sign-up → Brief → contract lock → paywall → purchase → research completion.
- Personally interview the first 25 purchasers. Ask: "What decision were you trying to make? What did you expect? Which evidence did you trust? What did you do next?"
- Respond to Play reviews promptly and professionally. Ask for feedback after value delivery, not at first open or before a result.

### Phase 2 — refine the winning wedge (weeks 5–12)

- Choose the best converting segment based on paid-pass conversion, result completion, refund/support rate, and 30-day return behavior — not installs alone.
- Build segment-specific pages and sample reports. For example, a mobile-app founder should see app-specific evidence and feasibility questions, while a service operator should see willingness-to-pay and workflow-change questions.
- Test one headline variable at a time: "evidence bar" vs. "decision trail" vs. "build/pivot/stop." Keep the workflow claim constant.
- Test Venture Pass price only after the experience and purchase instrumentation are stable; do not test price and positioning in the same experiment.

### Phase 3 — scale what earns trust (month 4+)

- Turn repeated founder questions into search content, templates, and in-product education.
- Establish a referral program only after a customer can honestly explain the value. Reward with an approved extension or account credit only if consistent with Play billing policy; do not use incentives that manipulate reviews.
- Expand through professional translations and localized screenshots after the English funnel reaches a measured baseline.
- Consider B2B/partner distribution only when the single-user privacy and workflow boundaries remain clear; do not imply team features that do not exist.

## 9. Measurement and decision rules

### Instrument the whole decision funnel

| Funnel step | Event | Why it matters | Initial diagnostic threshold, not a target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Play listing view / qualified landing-page visit | Is the message reaching relevant people? | Compare by channel and segment, not vanity volume. |
| Intent | Install / account creation | Is the promise compelling and credible? | Large drop-off means inspect listing-to-onboarding mismatch. |
| Activation | Idea saved | Did the intake feel approachable? | If low, simplify prompts and segment examples. |
| Method comprehension | Brief viewed and contract locked | Did users understand the distinctive mechanism? | This should be the primary early activation event. |
| Monetization | Paywall viewed → Venture Pass purchased | Does the paid boundary follow visible value? | Diagnose after users see the Brief, not before. |
| Delivery | Research completed | Did product delivery work and earn trust? | Investigate any failed/refunded passes immediately. |
| Value realization | Next action recorded / export / return session | Did the report change a decision or behavior? | More important than report-open rate. |
| Retention | Second research pass or repeat venture | Is the product supporting iteration without encouraging noise? | Interpret alongside user segment and pass balance. |

Track privacy-safe, aggregated product events. Do not put venture text, source URLs, purchase tokens, or sensitive research content into analytics events.

### North-star metric

**Weekly validated-decision rate:** unique users who complete a live research pass and record a next action (continue validation, interview, feasibility check, pivot, or stop) divided by users who create a Brief that week.

This rewards useful decisions, not spammy installs, endless report generation, or forced subscription behavior.

### Launch scoreboard

Use this weekly for the first 12 weeks. Keep the scoreboard simple enough that it changes decisions.

| Question | Metric | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Are the right people arriving? | Qualified visit to Brief-start rate by channel | Pause channels that create installs but few Briefs. |
| Is the promise clear? | Brief viewed to contract locked | If weak, fix intake copy, sample ideas, and the contract screen before buying more traffic. |
| Does the free boundary feel fair? | Contract locked to paywall viewed, paywall viewed to purchase | If many users lock but do not view/pay, clarify what the Brief includes and what live research adds. |
| Does paid delivery earn trust? | Purchase to completed research, refund/support rate | Fix product reliability before increasing acquisition spend. |
| Is the app changing behavior? | Result to next action recorded | If weak, improve post-result guidance and buyer-interview prompts. |
| Which segment should scale? | Paid research completions and next-action rate by segment | Scale the segment with decision completion, not just cheapest installs. |

### Guardrail metrics

- Research execution success and exact-once entitlement-restoration rate.
- Refund rate and refund reason.
- Time from Brief to contract lock, and from research result to a recorded next action.
- Support contacts per completed research pass.
- Share/export rate without exposing customer-sensitive content.
- Play rating/review themes, with qualitative review of the first 100 rather than overreacting to one comment.
- Channel-level payback only after enough purchases exist to make it meaningful.

### Experiments worth running

| Hypothesis | Variant A | Variant B | Winning signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founders value method transparency. | "Set the evidence bar before you build." | "Decide whether to build, pivot, or stop." | More contract locks per qualified visit, with no increase in immediate exits. |
| A sample makes the paid boundary clearer. | CTA to create a Brief | CTA to view a sample first | More paid research completions per new user, not merely more clicks. |
| The default offer needs project context. | Generic pricing page | Paywall naming the user's current idea family | More Venture Pass purchase with no higher refund rate. |
| The language "kill" attracts the right users but may repel some. | "Build the survivors." | "Make the next decision with evidence." | Better qualified activation and purchase, measured by next-action rate. |

Run one test per surface at a time, predefine the decision rule, and retain a control. Do not declare a winner on a handful of installs.

## 10. Credibility, legal, and product boundaries

1. **Truthful capability:** Do not market a feature before it is publicly usable. Google Play requires metadata and imagery to accurately describe the product and prohibits misleading claims. See [Google Play's deceptive-behavior policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17006354?hl=en&rd=1).
2. **No fabricated social proof:** Use only permissioned, attributed customer quotes. "Example founder reaction" belongs in a clearly labeled product demo/sample context, not a public claim that a customer said it.
3. **No result guarantee:** Include the plain-language limitation that Killgate is decision support, public research is not exhaustive, and customer conversations/real-world testing remain necessary.
4. **Protect founder confidentiality:** Request permission before publishing a use case, redact the idea, and offer an approval step for any case study. Never use private venture text in an ad audience, an email subject line, or a public example.
5. **Do not make billing the headline:** State the value first, make research-pass scope clear, and let Play show localized pricing at checkout.
6. **Avoid negative competitor comparison:** Do not name, screenshot, imply affiliation with, or make unprovable claims about other products. The comparison should be against weak methods — score-only judgment, untraceable output, and unexamined assumptions.

## Research note

This strategy used a current scan of founder-validation offerings on 30 August 2026 to identify recurring category promises: instant scores, broad AI reports, free scans, multi-artifact suites, speed claims, "confidence to build" language, investor-ready deliverables, live-source claims, founder-data claims, pivots, and generated business-plan packages. Their names and direct comparisons are intentionally excluded. The plan differentiates Killgate through product mechanics that are present in this repository, rather than unverified claims about the other products.

The public comparison should stay at the method level:

- Score-first products can be fast, but a score is hard to challenge if the evidence standard was not locked before the answer.
- Report-first products can feel complete, but long output does not automatically tell a founder what would disprove the idea.
- Build/launch suites can create momentum, but Killgate should own the prior decision: whether the idea deserves that build effort at all.
- Free-scan products can drive signups, but Killgate's cleaner promise is a free Brief with an explicit paid boundary for live evidence.
- Investor-document products can produce impressive artifacts, but Killgate should not pretend a document proves demand. It should help the founder decide what to test next.

Use this language internally for sales training and positioning. Do not paste it into Play metadata as a comparison against other apps.
