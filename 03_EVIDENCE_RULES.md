# Evidence Rules

## Evidence hierarchy

| Level | Name | What counts | What it can prove |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | Synthetic | AI role-play, model speculation, generated personas/surveys | Nothing about real demand |
| 1 | Market fact | Verified pricing, regulation, product pages, documented constraints | Market context |
| 2 | Observed | Real public user language/behavior, reviews, forum posts, issue threads | Observed problem/workaround patterns |
| 3 | Direct | Authentic interaction with a qualified buyer | Direct pain/workflow/budget reaction |
| 4 | Commitment | Costly action short of payment | Meaningful intent |
| 5 | Payment | Completed payment or verifiable paid deposit | Strong economic behavior |

Higher levels may strengthen a decision but lower levels cannot be relabeled to satisfy higher gates.

## Public-source relevance labels

These labels are separate from evidence levels:

- **DIRECT:** the source directly addresses the named buyer/problem/current behavior/alternative/pricing/spend/constraint.
- **INDIRECT:** adjacent context, but insufficient to substantiate a material hypothesis claim.
- **IRRELEVANT:** generic, keyword-only, reference-only, or unrelated content.

`DIRECT` public-source relevance does **not** mean Level 3 direct-buyer evidence.

## Never count as demand validation

- AI personas or simulated interviews;
- founder assertions about demand or urgency;
- generic compliments, likes, or “cool idea” reactions;
- “I would pay” without a real offer and observable action;
- dictionary/thesaurus/reference pages;
- Wikipedia as customer-demand evidence;
- generic social profiles;
- isolated keyword matches;
- duplicate/syndicated copies;
- seller testimonials without labeling commercial interest;
- competitor marketing copy as customer pain evidence.

## Independence and deduplication

- One person repeating the same point ten times = one independent voice for prevalence.
- Multiple comments by the same buyer in one thread = one voice for prevalence.
- Ten articles quoting the same study = one underlying source.
- Follow-up interviews with the same buyer = one buyer for unique-buyer counts.
- Multiple domains owned by or copying the same underlying source are not automatically independent.

## Payment verification

A payment counts only if all are true:

- status is paid;
- amount is present and > 0;
- a meaningful reference exists (invoice ID, receipt ID, processor reference, deposit record, etc.).

Do not accept placeholders such as `paid`, `yes`, `cash`, `done`, `receipt`, `n/a`, or `payment` as a meaningful reference by themselves.

## Concrete price-positive verification

A price-positive buyer counts only when:

- an actual non-zero pilot price was stated;
- the buyer's reaction to that exact price is recorded;
- the record is otherwise a qualified direct-buyer record.

A vague “sounds reasonable” with no concrete tested price does not count.
