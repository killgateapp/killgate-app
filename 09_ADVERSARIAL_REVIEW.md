# Adversarial Review and Falsification Loop

Run this before every material verdict.

## 1. Competing hypotheses

Generate at least three materially different explanations for the evidence when plausible. Examples:

- the pain is real and poorly served;
- the pain is real but current alternatives are already good enough;
- the public complaints are loud but economically trivial;
- the buyer has pain but is not the budget owner;
- the problem exists only in a narrow trigger condition;
- willingness to pay is for a service/outcome, not the proposed product form.

## 2. Attempt to falsify the leading explanation

Search specifically for:

- people saying the problem is rare or not important;
- satisfaction with current alternatives;
- failed competitors and why they failed;
- high switching costs;
- acquisition channels that are too fragmented or expensive;
- regulatory/platform restrictions;
- price resistance;
- workflow incompatibility;
- evidence that the apparent buyer is not the purchaser.

## 3. Evidence quality challenge

Ask:

- Is this source actually about the same buyer and job?
- Is this one person being counted multiple times?
- Is this seller-created material masquerading as user evidence?
- Did a search snippet get treated as verified source content?
- Did a hypothetical reaction get promoted into commitment/payment?
- Did the hypothesis change after evidence arrived?
- Is the conclusion relying on absence of evidence as evidence of absence?

## 4. Contradiction weighting

Do not average all evidence equally. Weight by:

1. evidence level;
2. source independence;
3. recency/context;
4. directness to the exact buyer/problem;
5. behavioral cost of the signal.

A verified payment can outweigh several weak verbal objections. Ten copied articles cannot outweigh one independent source merely through count.

## 5. Second-pass audit

Before answering, ask internally:

- What is the most likely thing I got wrong?
- What evidence would reverse my verdict?
- Am I rescuing the founder's preferred story?
- Am I killing an idea because search failed rather than because the market contradicted it?
- Did I apply the locked thresholds exactly?

Revise the conclusion when this audit exposes a real weakness.
